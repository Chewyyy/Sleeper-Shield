01
Foundation

Understand the app’s data language

Sleeper Shield separates four ideas that are often confused: projected points, player value, team fit, and confidence. A player can be highly valuable without being the best start this week, and a trade can be fair in market value while helping one roster much more than the other.

TermWhat it meansHow to use it
ProjectionExpected fantasy points from a weekly stat line or, when weekly data is unavailable, a season-rate fallback scored with your league settings.Use it for lineup decisions and near-term roster impact, not as a guarantee.
ValueA long-form asset score blending format-specific market data, league-scored production and projections, replacement value, efficiency, availability, and dynasty age when applicable.Use it to price trades and protect assets. Do not compare the number directly across unrelated leagues.
VORPValue over replacement. It measures projected scoring above the level normally available for that position in this exact league structure.Prioritize it at scarce starter positions, especially Superflex QB, premium TE, and deep IDP formats.
Team fitThe modeled change to starting-lineup output and depth after a move.Use it as the tiebreaker between similarly priced players or offers.
ConfidenceHow much verified market, projection, production, and league data supports the result.Demand a larger margin before acting on a low-confidence recommendation.
Projected pickA draft slot estimated from original-roster strength because Sleeper has not published an exact order.Treat the range as useful planning information, not a locked pick number.
League-specific means league-specific

Superflex, TE premium, IDP demand, flex slots, roster depth, scoring bonuses, and team count can materially change the same player’s value.

Two decimals are display precision

The interface limits visible numbers to two decimals for clarity. It does not imply that projections are certain to that precision.

02
Weekly decisions

Recommended Lineup

The lineup optimizer fills the league’s exact legal starter slots with the highest projected combination. It accounts for weekly projections, your scoring settings, opponent, byes, injury availability, reserve and taxi status, and the opportunity cost of using a flexible player in one slot instead of another.

How to read each starter row

  • Slot: the exact QB, RB2, FLEX, SUPER_FLEX, IDP, or other position being filled.
  • Recommended player: the optimizer’s best choice for that specific slot.
  • Upcoming-week projection: the points expected under the active league’s scoring.
  • Matchup label: whether the weekly projection is above or below the player’s season baseline.
  • Current Sleeper lineup: the player presently occupying that same slot. “Already set” means no change is needed.
  • Weekly Stats: the game log and usage evidence behind the recommendation.

Lineup strategy

  • Prefer the recommendation when the projection gap is meaningful and both players have similar injury certainty.
  • When the gap is small, use role stability, snap share, starts, and recent stat production as the tiebreaker.
  • Question a high projection paired with falling snaps or an uncertain injury designation.
  • During the offseason and preseason, the app deliberately prepares Week 1. Current-slot comparisons remain visible, but start/sit change totals wait for the regular-season lineup cycle.
Do not chase tiny differences

A projected edge of a few tenths can disappear with a role change, weather shift, or late injury report. The wider the uncertainty, the more projection separation you should require.

03
Market decisions

Trade Analyzer

Trade analysis begins with the market value of every player and pick, then applies package construction effects. It displays roster fit separately so a team need can explain why a fair deal is attractive without pretending that need changes the universal price of an asset.

  1. Confirm Team A.Your saved team defaults here. Team B should be the trade partner.
  2. Add the assets each team sends.Players and owned picks are available in each team’s picker. Current-year picks remain available before that rookie draft is completed.
  3. Read market fairness first.The verdict compares package-adjusted value and an uncertainty-based fair range.
  4. Read roster impact second.Projected starting-lineup and depth changes show whether the same trade solves a real problem.
  5. Inspect the player logs.Use weekly points, starts, snaps, and scoring events to test whether recent role supports the valuation.

Consolidation premium

Several replaceable assets usually should not equal one elite difference-maker dollar for dollar. The model applies elite scarcity and package consolidation before deciding fairness.

Suggested targets

Targets are ranked from the selected partner by positional need, projected lineup gain, VORP, and what that partner would lose by moving the player.

Generate Offer

The offer search tests one-, two-, and three-asset combinations from your players and picks, then prioritizes the fair range and both teams’ roster needs.

Negotiation edge

Start with the generated offer, then look for a version that fills the other manager’s clearest need. A fair offer that visibly improves both rosters is easier to complete than one that only balances the value meter.

04
Waiver decisions

Free Agency Analyzer

The Free Agency screen ranks only players who are unrostered in the active Sleeper league. It combines projected output, positional need, lineup improvement, depth, VORP, efficiency, and dynasty context, then models each possible pickup against specific drop candidates.

Pickup process

  1. Select your team and choose Best Available or a league-supported position. IDP combines defensive line, linebacker, and defensive back.
  2. Open Weekly Stats on a target to check ownership, starts, snaps, and how he actually earned points.
  3. Select Analyze pickup to see drop options.
  4. Compare market-value change, lineup change, and depth change together.

Drop discipline

  • Add: the swap improves the roster without sacrificing a more useful asset.
  • Marginal: the move is close enough that role trend, schedule, and roster flexibility should decide it.
  • Trade, do not drop: the cut candidate still has enough value that releasing him would waste an asset.
  • In dynasty, be especially careful with taxi players and young players whose immediate projection understates long-term value.
Roster percentage is context, not proof

A low roster rate can identify an overlooked player. A high roster rate can validate market interest. Neither replaces role, scoring fit, or the cost of the player you would drop.

05
Rookie planning

Draft Analyzer

The rookie board answers two different questions. The left side identifies the best overall fits for the selected roster. The right side estimates which players are likely to remain available at each pick the team actually owns.

Best fits

Fit rankings blend class quality, projection upside, draft capital signals, scoring, and positional need. Use this side to build a team-specific board before thinking about exact pick availability.

Likely at your picks

Availability follows traded-pick ownership and runs repeated, team-need-aware mock drafts. An exact pick has no prefix; a projected position begins with ~ until Sleeper publishes the order.

  • Do not pass on a clearly superior prospect solely because another position is a greater immediate need.
  • Use availability percentages to decide whether you can wait until a later pick or should move up.
  • Future picks are discounted because both timing and eventual draft position are uncertain.
  • Recheck the board after trades because team strength, needs, and pick ownership can all change.

08
Control room

Settings and My Team

League management

  • League IDs and My Team selections are saved on this device.
  • Each league keeps its own My Team setting.
  • Switching leagues applies that league’s saved default to Lineup, Trade Side A, Free Agency, and Draft.
  • Temporarily choosing another team in an analyzer does not overwrite My Team.
  • Export data creates a local snapshot. Clear saved leagues removes league and team-default preferences from this browser.

Model controls

  • Dynasty age and horizon: changes the influence of position-specific career timing. It is hidden in redraft.
  • Recent production: changes the influence of verified recent scoring.
  • Projection: changes the influence of expected future output.
  • Efficiency: changes the controlled influence of production relative to opportunity.
  • Team need: changes recommendation fit, not the underlying universal market price.
  • Draft-pick aggressiveness: changes how strongly the model prices rookie picks.
Change weights deliberately

Move one control at a time and observe several players or offers before keeping the change. Extreme settings can reflect your philosophy, but they do not create better underlying data.

09
Decision workflows

Use Sleeper Shield strategically

Weekly

Set the lineup

Review recommended changes, compare current-slot projections, inspect close calls in Weekly Stats, then confirm late injuries before kickoff.

Waivers

Buy role before points

Look for rising snap share and repeatable volume before the fantasy score fully arrives. Confirm that the proposed drop is truly expendable.

Trades

Separate price from purpose

First require fair market value. Then use lineup and depth impact to identify the version of the deal that best advances your roster plan.

Dynasty

Balance windows

Contenders can accept some age for immediate VORP; rebuilders should protect young elite assets and picks without treating every veteran as worthless.

Draft

Plan tiers, not one name

Use fit and availability together to identify several acceptable players at each pick and where a trade up or down creates value.

Uncertainty

Demand a margin

The lower the confidence or the more volatile the role, the larger the modeled advantage should be before you act.

Final check

Sleeper Shield uses public, read-only data. It cannot know private trade talks, breaking news that has not reached the feeds, contract or salary rules, off-platform assets, coaching intent, or your league mates’ personal preferences. Use the model as disciplined evidence, not automatic authority.

Methodology

How it works

Player value starts with format-specific Sleeper ADP, then blends league-scored projections, value over replacement, verified production, efficiency, age, and availability.

Draft pick value uses Sleeper’s exact slot when the order is set; otherwise it estimates early, middle, or late position from the original roster’s projected strength and applies a future-year discount.

Trade fairness applies elite-asset scarcity and package consolidation before comparing market value. Team fit is shown separately from price using each roster’s projected lineup and depth before and after the deal.

Free agency ranks unrostered players by team need and projected lineup impact, then compares each pickup against the roster’s lowest-cost drop options.

Recommended lineup scores weekly projected stat lines with the league’s exact settings, compares each player with his season baseline for matchup context, and solves for the highest-scoring legal starter combination.

Limitations: Sleeper Shield does not know private trade talks, breaking-news context, contract leagues, or off-platform draft capital. Use it as a decision aid, not a final authority.
